### Can AI Judges Serve as a Viable Alternative to Human Judges by Mitigating Their Inherent Limitations? ###

**Introduction**

Recently, academic interest in how to resolve distrust in the judiciary and adopting AI judges 

as a new viable alternative in the field has increased as human judges’ problems have revealed 

to the public through media. But, analyzing previous research, previous one tends to only focus on 

their technical analysis such as their algorithmic bias. 

Admittedly, technical possibility is important for evaluating whether it can serve a viable alternative. 

However, considering the critical characteristics of the legal field where it will be applied,

a comprehensive approach through legal philosophy is also equally important. 

Thus, this essay will analyze whether AI judges can serve as a viable alternative based on representative theories 

about the essence of law, natural law theory, and legal positivism.

Also, it will present technical, theoretical, and social views for designing AI judges 

so that they become the method to come as close as possible to the essence of law 

by mitigating human judges’ inherent limitations.

First of all, the sections that follow present two influential theories which explain the essence of law.

Ultimately, The findings of this study suggest that AI judges can serve as a viable alternative to human judges by mitigating their inherent limitations.

**The limitation of natural law theory in realizing the essence of law**

First representative theory for explaining the essence of law is Natural law theory. 

Main premise of this theory is to admit higher law than positive law. 

This higher law called as natural law. This theory became the basic evidence for punishing Nazi war criminals in Nuremberg Trials after World War ∥.

Ultimately, this theory connects to many opinions about integrating morality and law. 

Although this assertion seems to be principled and moral, there are serious problems in this.

The main weakness with this theory is that Natural law is not a specific clause, 

and moral criteria for judging incidents is different for each person. 

If legal professionals considered natural law theory as the basis one, 

one of the law’s important elements, objectivity, would disappear.

This system lacks the fundamental requirements of due process. Hart (1958) 

also criticizes Natural law theory, mentioning intellectual confusion of the public. :

"On the one hand Bentham had in mind the anarchist who argues thus: 

'This ought not to be the law, therefore it is not and I am free not merely to censure but to disregard it.'

On the other hand he thought of the reactionary who argues: 'This is the law, therefore it is what it ought

to be,' and thus stifles criticism at its birth" (Hart, 1958, pp. 597–598). 

To put it another way, There exist negative views about not only natural law theory’s essence but also social views. 

In other words, contrary to intuitive belief, integrating law and morality can make our society confused 

and we can know it is far from essence of law. Through these arguments, we can know necessity

of new theoretical approach for realizing ideal law.


**Legal Positivism : Upholding the Essence of Law through Objectivity**

Another theory for explaining the essence of law is legal positivism. 

Legal positivism is an academic view which admits only positive law as law. 

The strong point of this theory is objectivity. It interprets law as just statutes and prevents 

intervention of moral judgment which can distort the essence of law through subjectivity. 

In other words, this theory is exact opposite to natural law theory and it completely divides these two ideologies.

While many analysts now argue that the strategy of legal positivism cannot reflect our society or make our society righteous, 

a deeper analysis reveals that this approach safeguards the legal system. 

One of the theses which explains this theory comprehensively is Hart (1958).

Hart is one of the giant legal philosophers in 20th century and he is considered as representative scholar of 20th-century legal positivism.

He presented this thesis in 1958 when the separation of law and morals was criticized by public due to impact of Nazi.

Nazi criminals asserted they just followed the German law at that time, 

so there is no evidence for punishing them. Although they were found guilty in Nuremberg Trials, public’s distrust about legal positivism

remained the same. So, Hart wrote this thesis for changing this atmosphere made by public and legal professionals,

this is evaluated as prestigious thesis. The main concept is core and penumbra, 

admitting the minimum of natural law when establishing law for survival. First, As Hart(1958) asserts : 

"there must be a core of settled meaning, but there will be, as well, a penumbra of debatable cases in which

words are neither obviously applicable nor obviously ruled out" (1958, p. 607). there are two parts in

law. Core is the certain parts which can make judgment based on law. 

And, penumbra is the uncertain parts which can not make judgment instantly through law.

In this situation, judicial judgment is necessary. But, this is not application of morality. 

Judges just regard law as clause and makes judgments considering previous judicial precedents, 

the situation when the law is established etc. In other words, it means human’s judgment is just mechanical decision for resolving problems

made by penumbra parts not moral decision. Second, Hart admits the minimum of natural law when establishing law : 

"If we are to live together and make use of one another in the ways which morality and law make possible,

we must submit to the restrictions which are the minimum content of natural law" (Hart, 1958, p. 622). 

It is for our lives as we will be dangerous if the law can not guarantee our safety.

But, this permission is applied only for the process of making law. 

And the situation when morality affect legal judgment after making law considered as distortion of essence of law.

Also, Hart asserts moral judgment can be applied. But, this is only for the time when establishing law and the

law evaluated as not being moral is also law: "The moral iniquity of such laws would be something

that could be demonstrated; it would surely follow merely from a statement of what the rule required to

be done that the rule was morally wrong and so ought not to be law or conversely that it was morally

desirable and ought to be law. But the demonstration of this would not show the rule not to be (or to

be) law" (Hart, 1958, p. 626). And, Hart presents two advantages which legal positivism has, 

they are clarity: "So perhaps the most important single lesson to be learned from this form of the denial of the

Utilitarian distinction is the one that the Utilitarians were most concerned to teach: when we have the

ample resources of plain speech we must not present the moral criticism of institutions as

propositions of a disputable philosophy." (Hart, 1958, p. 621). and sharper foundation for moral

criticism: "If with the Utilitarians we speak plainly, we say that laws may be law but too evil to be

obeyed. This is a moral condemnation which everyone can understand and it makes an immediate

and obvious claim to moral attention." (Hart, 1958, p. 620). 

These advantages demonstrate why legal positivism remains a robust framework for judicial objectivity. 

These are contexts of Hart’s theory.

Hart’s framework is considered as one of the highly effective application methods of legal positivism

to reality. However, the previously mentioned methods suffer from some serious drawbacks in reality,

such as human judges’ inherent limitations, application of other ineffective methods 

related to legal positivism to reality instead of effective methods such as Hart’s one. 

In the section that follows, it will be argued that there are many problems in reality

although legal positivism is evaluated as the robust theory towards the essence of law.

**The problems caused by human judges**
As mentioned in previous paragraph, there are many practical problems in reality although the theory
is a robust framework. It is because there are numerous restrictions in reality to operate theory’s
concept correctly. Among them, the representative things are human judges’ adoption of ineffective
interpretations of legal positivism and their inherent limitation related to unconscious bias. Human
judges’ adoption of ineffective method becomes the hot issues especially in USA. Detailed situation
can be found in Collins and Komatsu (2025) and Wolf (2024). These two sources both criticize
originalism strongly. Originalism is the extreme version of legal positivism and it only considers the
original legislative intent and the situation at the time of law's enactment. This application method
differs from Hart’s method, Hart’s one is distinguished from originalism as that it considers not only
the original legislative intent but also previous judicial precedent etc. First, the problem of originalism
presented in those two sources is commonly that it can’t reflect current society enough : "The simple
fact that originalism forces courts to look to the past for binding rules for dealing with today’s
questions accounts for some of its regressive impact" (Wolf, 2024, p. 2). This reliance on past
directives illustrates that originalism often operates on outdated ideological premises. One of the
representative examples is historical changes about women's suffrage. Although laws about
restricting women’s vote right is deleted, if the other law about different field is established at the time
when women’ vote right is restricted and it exists until now, originalism which only considers the
situation at the time of law’s enactment can be evaluated as outdated methods which can not reflect
current society. Even, the key point is human judges misuse this theory for justifying their judgments.
Other authors (see Collins & Komatsu, 2025; Wolf, 2024) question the usefulness of originalism
arguing that human judges misuse this theory through historical distortion: "However, conservative
Junghwa Kim

lawyers and judges have also distorted the past they use to support their originalist arguments,
reading out of the historical record the progressive potential of the Constitution, certain moments in
history, or older ways of thinking." (Wolf, 2024, p. 2). and specific conservative
organization(Conservative Federalist Society) is misusing originalism for making biased judgments
related to conservative ideology : "We found that justices connected to the Federalist Society are
about 10 percentage points more likely to cast a conservative vote than other justices, even other
justices appointed by Republican presidents." (Collins & Komatsu, 2025, p. 4). In other words, it
indicates some human judges tend to not only adopt ineffective theories but also misuse these
frameworks. The limitation of this approach is also that the research about that human judges can
have an inherent limitation related to unconscious bias is continuously reported. It means human
judges can have unconscious bias although they tried not to be biased when making judgments.
Actually, Irwin and Real (2011) analyzed whether people who have diverse jobs have unconscious
bias through IAT test which can measure unconscious bias of people. Test results were different with
participant’s evaluation about themselves. It is revealed that many people who consider they don’t
have any bias actually have unconscious bias: "Test takers who believe that they do not have
negative biases toward, for example, a particular race, gender, or age group, 'are nevertheless likely
to be slower to associate' the 'good' words with those groups, than they are to associate other groups
with 'good' words." (Irwin & Real, 2016, p. 3). Even, it is also revealed that doctors have unconscious
bias when administering medication to a patient: "Alexander Green et al., Implicit Bias Among
Physicians and its Prediction of Thrombolysis Decisions for Black and White Patients, 22 J. GEN.
INTERNAL MED. 1231 (suggesting that racial bias affects the treatment that many African-American
patients receive in hospital emergency rooms)" (Irwin & Real, 2016, p. 2, n.3). This results suggest
that human in diverse jobs even judges can have unconscious bias although they tried not to be
biased. In other words, human judges not only can adopt ineffective methods or misuse the theory but
also can be biased unconsciously even if they try hard not to be biased. In this situation, we can
consider AI judges as a alternative. So, in the following section, we will deal with the context about
whether AI judges can be a viable alternative to human judges by mitigating their inherent limitations.
An Evaluation of AI Judges as a Potential Alternative to Human Judges
As previously analyzed, human judges’ problems stem from human’s subjectivity. To delete
subjectivity, AI can be a potential alternative as it is operated based on their algorithms. The fact that
AI operates on defined algorithm informs us the judgment is predictable and not subjective. Also, we
can design its engine so that they follow the effective application methods which can realize the
essence of law. These are merits exclusive to machines, and it is consistent with the essence of law.
However, Critics question the ability of AI algorithms to provide unbiased judgments, as AI is not a
perfect technology. But, There are potentials for resolving these critics questions in AI as it is just
based on their algorithms and operates mechanically. Also, the design of AI algorithms is critical to
prevent AI from making judgments which distort the value of law. Although developing fair algorithms
requires rigorous discussion, considering the essence of law, moderate theory based on legal
positivism like Hart’s one will be appropriate instead of originalism. Furthermore, recently, new
theories such as popular originalism are presented. For example, popular originalism is explained in
Donnelly (2025), it means integrating originalism and living constitutionalism which considers
constitution as living organism and using successive filters when making judgment: "By introducing
'popular originalism,' this framework effectively 'reconcile[s] originalism with certain forms of living
constitutionalism,' utilizing a methodology where 'originalists may choose between six different
interpretive perspectives'—acting as a 'series of successive filters'—to address constitutional
indeterminacy when the text alone does not provide a single right answer" (Donnelly, 2025, p. 1597).
Admittedly, this kind of theory also can be misused in reality. Major criticism of Balkin's theory which is
similar to popular originalism, made by Novak (2025), is that this kind of integrating theory can also be
misused in reality: "The ever-expanding menu of interpretive options results in something Balkin calls
'cafeteria originalism,' where legal advocates get to 'pick and choose when to follow the views of the
founders, framers, or adopters . . . and often artfully recharacterize these principles to support
contemporary political and legal arguments'" (Novak, 2025, p. 1194). In other words, it warns
integrating theory can be also misused like originalism. So, for resolving Novak’s criticism and
minimizing AI judges’ distortion of the essence of law, a multifaceted approach involving technical,
academic, and social perspectives is required. Javed and Li (2025) deal with this context in detail.
This thesis emphasizes our social role for making AI judges not be misused with simulation results
from short term to long term when adopting AI judges. Simulation results indicate AI judges will be
helpful for our society although it makes biased judgement in short-term: "In summary, while AI and
media introduce biases in the short to medium term, both have the potential to support the JS
positively in the long run." (Javed & Li, 2025, p. 26). Also, it emphasizes necessity of monitoring by
media and public as AI is not a perfect technology: "Therefore, even though AI has the potential to
improve the fairness and accuracy of case resolutions, its long-term efficacy in reducing prejudice is
still dependent on a number of variables. [...] which emphasizes the necessity of constant critical
monitoring, openness, and continual learning in AI systems employed in judicial contexts." (Javed &
Li, 2025, p. 30). This is meaningful as it indicates the possibility about AI judges will be helpful for our
society and it suggests the necessity of our social role as monitor. In other words, we should always
remember AI is not perfect technology and we should monitor them not to be misused by specific
organization. Also, technical and academic perspective are important. As mentioned above, academic
discussion is essential for making fair algorithm. Academic consensus will be necessary as
algorithm’s theory is the basic of AI judges. Technical advances are also crucial. Although it is not
covered in this essay, for making faster, more accurate and less biased judgments, technical
advances are an essential element. If these three factors are fulfilled, It is expected that AI judges can
be a viable alternative to human judges also preventing Novak’s concerns about misusing. Until now,
we evaluated AI judges whether it can be a viable alternative to human judges with technical,
academic and social perspectives. Overall, AI judges can be evaluated as a viable alternative to
human judges by mitigating their inherent limitations if previous three factors were completed.
